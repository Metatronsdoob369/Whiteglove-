WhiteGlove gate calibration — how the threshold was chosen.

A threshold is not a constant; it is a measurement with provenance. The
operating gate of 0.325 was chosen as the last zero-false-answer point on a
sweep over a 162-query corpus: grounded queries that must answer with the
expected evidence, ungrounded queries that must stay silent, and adversarial
near-misses that swap one entity or version number to tempt a false answer.

The eval harness scores five outcomes: true_answer, wrong_evidence,
false_silence, true_silence, and false_answer. The headline number is the
false_answer rate — hallucination under absence — measured against a naive
baseline with the gate disabled. Calibration only counts when the tokenizer,
the IDF weighting, and the corpus are named alongside the number, because a
threshold measured under one distance distribution does not transfer to
another.

Two flagged values remain deliberately uncalibrated: the 0.45 medical pin
predates the code-aware tokenizer, and the 0.2858 shard-drift threshold is
scheduled for recalibration. Neither may gate live inference until re-swept.
