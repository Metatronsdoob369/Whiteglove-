## 1) What the temporal-spectral architecture unlocks (beyond standard RAG)

Most retrieval tools answer: "What is closest in text space?"  
WhiteGlove answers that and also tracks how knowledge changes over time.

In practical terms, this means you can see:
- What is stable doctrine vs. what is actively changing
- Where drift is accelerating
- Where contested zones exist vs. low-risk stable zones

This is not just better search. It is a structured map of institutional knowledge motion.

## 3) The bigger idea: canonical data architecture

WhiteGlove is not just a retrieval product. It is a method for turning any document corpus into a navigable, auditable knowledge topology.

Potential applications:
- Regulatory agencies: identify unsettled zones before issuing guidance
- Insurance: detect high-drift areas before pricing risk
- M&A due diligence: find contradiction density in internal corpora early
- Clinical research: separate settled evidence from active debate before study design
- National security / OSINT: use temporal drift as an early signal layer
- Education: teach what is stable vs. truly open in a field

The moat is not the chatbot surface. The moat is the ability to fingerprint a corpus' epistemic state: what it knows, what changed, and where uncertainty remains.
